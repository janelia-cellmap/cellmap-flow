cellmap_flow.finetune.trainer.supervised

The supervised term of the training loss, and how the model’s output is read for it.

SupervisedLoss builds the criterion loss_type names and compares the model’s output with the target where it is annotated. probe_output_class reads what the output is – a sigmoid baked in, a tanh, or logits – when nothing declared it, in the classes the model’s server reports (serving.probe), so that training, ranking and seeding read one model the same way. The browser’s twin is web/src/finetune/full/loss.ts.

cellmap_flow.finetune.trainer.supervised.logger
cellmap_flow.finetune.trainer.supervised.probe_output_class(model, example, select_channel=None) → str

What model’s output is – unit, signed_unit or unbounded (serving.probe) – read off its answer to an extreme input.

example is an input batch of the shape the model takes; the probe is noise of that shape at 100x, so that an output bounded by a sigmoid or a tanh shows its bounds and logits show their range. select_channel reads the channel training compares. The model is probed in eval mode and left as it was: in train mode a full finetune’s BatchNorm took the noise into its running statistics – about 7,000x on every channel, which takes ~85 batches to decay, longer than most interactive runs – and the export and the served model normalize by them. A probe that fails reads as logits, with a warning.

class cellmap_flow.finetune.trainer.supervised.SupervisedLoss(options, target_transform=None)

The supervised term: the criterion options.loss_type names, compared with the target where it is annotated.

What the criterion sees depends on what the model’s output is (options.output_class): probabilities when it is bounded – a sigmoid or a tanh baked in, where a second sigmoid would squash [0, 1] into [0.5, 0.73] and make a well-fitting prediction look like a constant – so BCE-with-logits becomes BCE and Dice and margin skip their sigmoid; logits otherwise (losses.as_probabilities). MSE always compares probabilities with the 0/1 target, as dice and margin do: on a logit model it used to compare the raw logits, training them toward 0 and 1, which the served sigmoid turns into 0.5 and 0.73, wrecking any threshold. The interval loss bounds the logit, where a distance field is a line; the flow loss reads flows and a logit as the model emits them, whatever the probe said (its flows can fall within [0, 1]).

Label smoothing is redundant with the margin loss and means nothing to bounds, flows or a soft distance target, so it is 0 for those; class balancing does not apply to the flow loss.

targets makes a batch’s target, mask and bounds from its annotation; the call computes the term and keeps the step’s metrics (the interval terms; on BCE the target’s entropy floor and the mean |p - t|), which tally sums over the epoch’s supervised batches.

targets(annotation)

A batch’s (target, mask, bounds, hard_target) from its annotation.

The target transform’s target and mask; for the interval loss the target is the bounds, and the painted class stands in as the target for the class check, the class balance and the TensorBoard image. Without a transform, the legacy reading: 0 unannotated, 1 background, 2 foreground, shifted down by one, and masked when mask_unannotated. hard_target is the target as annotated: class balancing splits voxels into fg and bg by it, before smoothing – split by the smoothed target, every bg voxel carried s/2 of fg weight (with 100 fg voxels against 100k bg at s = 0.1, 98% of the “fg” half of the loss was bg). Smoothing then moves 0 to s/2 and 1 to 1 - s/2.
