cellmap_flow.inference.runner
=============================

.. py:module:: cellmap_flow.inference.runner

.. autoapi-nested-parse::

   Running a model on a region: the device, the warmup and the forward.

   ``ModelRunner`` puts a ModelConfig's model on the device, reads its output
   activation off its network's graph (``ModelConfig.output_graph``,
   ``portable.activation``), warms it up (the same forward checks the declared
   shapes and probes the output's range, which says the activation when the
   graph does not), and
   predicts an output region from a ``RawInput`` (``cellmap_flow.io.raw``; in
   the server, its ``BlockInput``): the model's raw output, ``(C, *spatial)``,
   with no postprocessing and no process-wide state.
   ``cellmap_flow.inferencer.Inferencer`` adds the normalization and
   postprocessing chain on top.

   ``DeviceSlots`` is how many chunks may use the device at once, and in what
   order; the inference server passes one, blockwise workers none.

   Importing this module is cheap: torch is imported when it is first needed.

.. py:function:: smooth_field(coordinates) -> numpy.ndarray

   A smooth synthetic image in [-1, 1] over ``coordinates``, one 1-D array
   of world positions (nm) per axis: a sum of sinusoid products at three
   wavelengths, a function of position alone, so a voxel reads the same in
   any tile that holds it. It varies over a few voxels of 4 to 16 nm data,
   so a network's output varies over it too, where random noise saturates
   most networks and zeros give a constant.


.. py:function:: disagreement(reference, output)

   How a tile's ``output`` compares with the native tile's ``reference``
   over the voxels they share, both ``(C, *spatial)`` arrays, ``output`` the
   bigger and centred on ``reference``: ``(correlation, difference, wrong)``.

   The correlation is Pearson's between the reference-sized centre of
   ``output`` and ``reference``, None when either is flat. The difference
   is their mean absolute difference in units of the reference's spread
   (its standard deviation, at least 1e-3); infinite when the centre has a
   non-finite value. ``wrong`` is whether the output is another answer
   rather than the same one slightly offset: the difference is past
   MAX_DISAGREEMENT, or the centre is flat where the reference varies (a
   constant, whatever its value), or not finite. ``(None, 0.0, False)``
   when there is nothing to compare: no reference, or shapes that do not
   nest.


.. py:class:: DeviceSlots(n=1)

   Lets at most ``n`` chunks use the device at once, in the order they asked.

   The inference server answers every request on its own thread, and
   neuroglancer asks for 6 chunks at once over HTTP/1.1, or dozens through an
   HTTP/2 proxy. Unbounded, k concurrent forwards interleave on the GPU and
   finish together, after about k forwards' time, so the first chunks of a
   view all appeared at once, late. Each forward also holds its own
   activations, and a dozen at once ran an 11 GB card out of memory. One
   slot makes the GPU compute chunks one after another, the first one asked
   for first, at the same total throughput: reading and normalizing the next
   chunk, which stay outside the slot, overlap the current forward.

   Strictly first come, first served: a thread that arrives while a slot is
   free still waits behind anyone already waiting. A plain Semaphore lets
   newcomers barge ahead.

   The server takes ``n`` from ``CELLMAP_FLOW_GPU_SLOTS`` (default 1) when it
   starts. More than one slot only helps a model too small to keep the
   device busy on its own.

   Neuroglancer drops the requests for chunks that have left the view after
   a pan. Dropping those chunks before they reach a slot is the server's
   queue's job (chunkmirage's), which admits chunks to the ops in the order
   they were asked for and drops those nobody waits for any more; a chunk
   already on the device finishes.

.. py:function:: predict(read_roi, write_roi, config, **kwargs)

   The default forward: read ``read_roi`` from ``kwargs["idi"]``, run
   ``config.model`` on ``kwargs["device"]``, return ``(C, *spatial)``.

   ``idi`` is what the model reads from: a ``RawInput``
   (``cellmap_flow.io.raw``), or the server's ``BlockInput``, anything with
   ``to_ndarray_ts(roi)``; the keyword keeps its old name, which scripts
   read. A script may define its own ``predict`` with this signature
   instead; it is then given ``idi``, ``device`` and ``use_half_prediction``.
